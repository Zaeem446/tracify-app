How we evaluated each app

Every app on this list was tested between January and April 2026 on both an iPhone 16 and a Pixel 9 Pro. We measured:

2. Apple Find My — Best for Apple device owners

#2

How it works: Built into every iPhone, iPad, Mac, and AirTag. Uses a combination of GPS, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and Apple's Find My network (a mesh of hundreds of millions of Apple devices that can relay the location of an offline device). Access it through the Find My app or icloud.com/find.

Price: Free.

Pros

  • Extremely accurate (within a few meters)
  • Works even when the phone is offline (via the Find My network)
  • Can play a sound, lock, or erase the device remotely
  • Family Sharing lets you see family members' locations
  • Completely free

Cons

  • Only works with Apple devices
  • Only tracks devices signed into your Apple ID or Family Sharing group
  • Cannot track someone else's phone by number

Our take: If you've lost your own iPhone or want to share locations within your Apple household, Find My is unbeatable. But it can't locate a phone that isn't tied to your Apple account, which is the most common use case people are searching for.

3. Google Find My Device — Best for Android device owners

#3

How it works: Google's equivalent to Apple's Find My. Works on any Android phone signed into a Google account. Access it via the Find My Device app or google.com/android/find. Since 2024, Google has also rolled out a crowd-sourced Find My Device network similar to Apple's, using Bluetooth signals from nearby Android devices.

Price: Free.

Pros

  • Free and pre-installed on all Android phones
  • Good accuracy with GPS and Wi-Fi triangulation
  • Remote ring, lock, and erase
  • New crowd-sourced network improves offline tracking

Cons

  • Android only
  • Only works for your own Google account devices
  • Less reliable than Apple's Find My when phone is offline

Our take: Same verdict as Find My. Perfect for your own lost phone. Useless for tracking someone else's number. If the person you want to locate uses Android and isn't in your Google family group, you need a different tool.

4. Life360 — Best for families who all opt in

#4

How it works: Life360 is a family location-sharing app. Every member installs the app on their phone, joins a shared "circle," and everyone in the circle can see everyone else's live location on a map. The app also includes driving safety features, crash detection, and roadside assistance on premium tiers.

Price: Free tier (basic location sharing), Silver at $9.99/month, Gold at $14.99/month, Platinum at $24.99/month.

Pros

  • Continuous real-time location sharing
  • Good for families with mutual buy-in
  • Geofencing alerts (notified when someone arrives or leaves a location)
  • Crash detection on paid plans

Cons

  • Everyone must install the app and create an account
  • Battery drain from constant background location tracking
  • Privacy concerns — Life360 was caught selling precise location data to data brokers in 2021
  • Teens frequently find ways to spoof their location or disable the app

Our take: Life360 works well when the whole family genuinely wants to share locations. The problem is that it requires everyone to install an app and keep it running, which creates friction. The data-selling controversy also raises serious privacy questions. If you just need an occasional check-in rather than 24/7 tracking, Tracify's SMS approach is simpler and less invasive.

5. mSpy — Powerful but legally problematic

#5

How it works: mSpy is a monitoring app installed directly on the target phone. Once installed, it runs invisibly in the background, sending GPS location, text messages, call logs, social media activity, browser history, and even keystrokes to a web dashboard. On iPhone, a "no-jailbreak" option uses iCloud credentials instead of direct installation.

Price: $49.99/month (Premium plan). Cheaper with annual billing.

Pros

  • Extremely comprehensive monitoring (far beyond location)
  • Real-time GPS tracking with geofencing
  • Works on both iOS and Android

Cons

  • Requires physical access to install (or iCloud credentials for iPhone)
  • Installing on someone else's phone without consent is illegal in most countries
  • Detected by Google Play Protect and most antivirus apps
  • Expensive at $50/month
  • Has been breached multiple times (2015, 2018), exposing customer data

Our take: mSpy is technically capable but carries serious legal risks. It's marketed for "parental monitoring," but the covert nature of the installation means it's frequently used as stalkerware. Unless you're monitoring your own child's phone with their knowledge, we recommend against it. For more on the legal risks, see our guide on phone tracking legality.

6. Cocospy — mSpy clone with a different brand

#6

How it works: Cocospy operates almost identically to mSpy. Install a hidden app on the target Android device, or provide iCloud credentials for iPhones. The dashboard shows location history, messages, calls, and social media activity. The company markets itself as a "parental control" tool.

Price: $49.99/month for the Premium plan. Basic plan starts at $39.99/month but lacks key features.

Pros

  • Clean dashboard interface
  • Includes location history timeline
  • No jailbreak needed for iOS (uses iCloud sync)

Cons

  • Same legal risks as mSpy — covert installation is illegal without consent
  • Requires physical access to the target Android phone
  • iCloud method requires the target's Apple ID and password
  • Questionable corporate transparency — company details are hard to verify
  • Auto-renewal billing that's difficult to cancel

Our take: Cocospy is essentially mSpy with a different coat of paint. Same category, same risks, same legal problems. If you're considering Cocospy for anything other than monitoring your own minor child's phone with their knowledge, reconsider.

7. Spyic — Similar approach, similar concerns

#7

How it works: Another stalkerware-category app. Hidden installation on Android, iCloud-based monitoring for iPhone. Features include GPS tracking, call and SMS monitoring, social media surveillance, and a keylogger. Markets itself as a "phone monitoring solution."

Price: $49.99/month (Premium). Annual plans bring the monthly cost down to around $10/month, but you're locked in for a year.

Pros

  • Full device monitoring suite
  • SIM card change alerts
  • Annual pricing makes it cheaper long-term

Cons

  • All the same legal and ethical issues as mSpy and Cocospy
  • Heavily reliant on positive "reviews" from affiliate sites, making independent evaluation difficult
  • Android app requires disabling Play Protect to install
  • No verifiable security audit history

Our take: Spyic, Cocospy, and mSpy are functionally interchangeable. They all require covert access to the target phone, they all operate in a legal grey zone (or outright illegally), and they all charge roughly the same. The phone tracking market would be better off without this category entirely.

8. FlexiSpy — Most expensive, most invasive

#8

How it works: FlexiSpy is the "premium" stalkerware option. Beyond standard GPS and message monitoring, it offers live call interception, ambient recording (turning the phone's microphone on remotely), and camera activation. Installation requires either rooting (Android) or jailbreaking (iPhone).

Price: $68/month for Premium, $199/month for Extreme (which includes call interception and ambient listening).

Pros

  • Most feature-rich monitoring tool available
  • Live call listening and ambient recording (Extreme plan)
  • Detailed analytics and reporting

Cons

  • Requires root/jailbreak — voids the phone's warranty and opens security vulnerabilities
  • Most expensive option on this list by a wide margin
  • Call interception and ambient recording are wiretapping — a serious felony in most jurisdictions
  • Targeted by law enforcement and advocacy groups as a stalking tool

Our take: FlexiSpy crosses the line from monitoring into full-blown surveillance. Call interception is a felony under the US Wiretap Act and equivalent laws in most countries. This is not a consumer product — it's a liability. We cannot recommend it under any circumstances.
